The idea in 60 seconds

A popular way to teach a robot is behaviour cloning: record a person doing the task, then train a network to map what the robot sees to what the person did. The simplest version is regression: minimise the squared error between the network's action and the demonstrated one.

The trouble is that people solve the same situation in different ways. Here half the demos go around the obstacle on the left and half on the right, both perfectly good. For squared error the best single answer is the average of the two, and the average of "go left" and "go right" is "go straight", into the obstacle. The data is multimodal, and regression can only output one point.

Diffusion Policy instead treats the action as something to generate, the way image diffusion models generate pictures. It starts from random noise and uses a learned denoiser, conditioned on the observation, to refine it step by step into a clean action. At high noise the denoiser's best guess is still the blurry average, but each step adds detail, and the small random differences decide which mode a sample falls into. Because it learns the whole distribution of actions, every sample commits to left or right, never the impossible middle.

The paper also has the policy predict a sequence of future actions at once, not one step at a time. This keeps the robot consistent within a mode instead of dithering between left and right. Here every plan is a short sequence of 8 waypoints, generated together.

Try this

  • Watch training: early diffusion samples are a scribble; within seconds they split into two clean families of paths, while the red regression plan heads straight for the obstacle.
  • Drag the Denoising step slider to 20, then slowly back to 0. Dots are the noisy plan; lines are the denoiser's current guess of the clean plan. At high noise every guess is the straight average route, exactly like regression. As noise is removed, each sample commits to the left or the right.
  • Drag the start to the far left or right of the start zone. Regression may sneak past at the edges, but in front of the obstacle it crashes every time. Panel 2 shows why: its single answer sits between the two humps.
  • Switch to 100% left. With one mode in the data, regression works fine. Its problem is averaging modes, not being too weak a model.

How this demo simplifies the paper

  • The "robot" is a point in 2D, the observation is just its start position, and the demonstrations are generated by a script (curves around the left or right of the disc), not recorded from people or images. The paper conditions on camera images and robot state and runs real robots and simulated benchmarks like Push-T.
  • Each policy plans the whole route as one sequence of 8 waypoints and executes it open loop. The paper predicts shorter action sequences (e.g. 16 steps), executes about 8 of them, then observes again and re-plans (receding horizon).
  • The denoiser is a 2-hidden-layer MLP (64 units) that predicts the clean action directly, with 20 diffusion steps, trained for 2,500 Adam steps in your browser. The paper uses a 1D temporal U-Net or a transformer that predicts the noise, ~100 training steps of noise, and faster DDIM sampling at test time.
  • The red baseline is plain mean-squared-error regression with the same network size, to make the averaging problem obvious. The paper's actual baselines are stronger multimodal methods (LSTM-GMM, IBC, BET); it reports Diffusion Policy beating them, which this toy does not reproduce.
